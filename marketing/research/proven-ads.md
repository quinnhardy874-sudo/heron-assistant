# What Christmas Light Ads Actually Work: Research Before Making Ours

Researched 2026-10-08 from the Meta Ad Library (live US ads) and published benchmark reports. Every number below has a source. Where a number is my own count, the method is stated.

## How "known to work" was measured
Meta doesn't publish other advertisers' results. The best public signal is **how long an ad keeps running**: advertisers turn off ads that lose money and keep the ones that make money. I also counted which hooks show up most across live ads. Common doesn't prove something works, but it does show what the market has converged on.

**Sample:** 3 Ad Library searches ("roofline lights", "Christmas light installation", "C9 Christmas lights"), US, run 2026-10-08. After removing off-topic ads and duplicate variants, that left **41 unique headlines from 43 different advertisers**.

## Finding 3 (the big one for Heron): 42% of these advertisers are lawn and exterior companies, like you

Of the 43 Christmas-light advertisers in the sample, **18 (42%) are landscaping, lawn-care, pressure-washing or exterior-cleaning companies** that add holiday lighting in Q4. Examples: Davidson Landscaping, Jabco's Custom Lawn Care, Augusta Lawn Care, 4 Seasons Outdoor Living, Cultivate House Detailing. One of them is **local to you: [Chesapeake ProWash → "Chesapeake ProLights"](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=847464595090439)**.

Meanwhile, **only 1 advertiser in the sample (The Christmas Light Emporium) sells a DIY roofline kit.**

**What this means:** "lawn company installs Christmas lights, local, offer-led" is a crowded but proven model. Dozens of businesses run it every year because it makes money. The DIY-kit model hardly anyone advertises, which could mean an open lane, or it could mean the market is small. **Heron Holiday Lighting is the safer bet for ad money this season. Roofline Kit is the experiment.**

## What this tells us to build

### Heron Holiday Lighting (local installs): the proven play
- **Format:** a real before-and-after video of a Towson house (dark, then lit), 10–15 seconds, shot vertically. Open on the lit house, not the product (the hook-rate data above).
- **Headline:** combine the two biggest hooks: an offer plus skip the ladder. For example: *"Skip the Ladder. $100 Off October Installs, Towson."*
- **Offer:** a real October discount or a free quote, with a real deadline (Oct 31). That's 34% offer-led and 12% deadline wording in the market.
- **Objective:** Leads (instant form) or Messages. Benchmark is about $38–41 per lead. With an install averaging $750–1,500, even a 1-in-5 close rate pays.
- **Area:** about 15 miles around Towson. Name the town in the headline.
- **Images:** real Heron jobs only. If you don't have any yet, light up your own house or one customer's at cost this week and film it.

### Roofline Kit (DIY kit): test, using the competitor's proven angle
- **Lead with "made to order + limited build capacity"**, which is the competitor's 51-day survivor. Our ad G already says this. Ad E's "pay once" line should be a supporting point, not the headline.
- **Second angle: labeled runs (ad F).** No competitor says it, so it's your unique point, but it's untested.
- **Format:** a 15-second phone video: hands pull a labeled run from the box, clip it on a gutter, then cut to the lit house at night. It's real footage (UGC style) and video, which are the two best-supported levers above.
- **Retire AI images** before spending. Every benchmark above favors real or customer-made content, and AI images are likely to get Meta's "AI info" label.

## Bottom line
1. **Put the ad money into Heron Holiday Lighting first.** It's a proven model (42% of light advertisers are businesses like yours) with known costs (about $40 per lead).
2. Roofline Kit gets $5/day only after you have real photos or video, and it leads with "made to order, limited capacity."
3. Everything hinges on **real footage of a real house.** Shoot it this week.
