## 0. Read this first: what the data can and can't tell you

Be clear on these limits before you act on anything below.

1. **The Meta Ad Library tool returns only the page name, the headline (link title), the creation date and the delivery-start date for each ad.** It does not return body copy, format (video/static/carousel), CTA button, landing URL or end date. Every "hook" quoted below is the ad's **headline**, not its first line or its first 3 seconds of video. Format and CTA are marked *not observed* wherever they apply. To see the creative, open the `ad_snapshot_url` links (`facebook.com/ads/library/?id=…`) in a browser. I couldn't open them because facebook.com is blocked from this environment.
2. **Results come back newest-first, capped at 50 per query, with no pagination.** For big advertisers like Govee (388 active) and JellyFish (904 active), I only saw the 50 newest. So "longest-running" means the oldest ad I could see. It does not mean the oldest ad they have running.
3. **"Active since" dates are only reliable for queries I ran with `ad_active_status=ACTIVE`,** and those are marked. Ads from ALL-status queries may already have stopped.
4. **Most competitor websites were blocked for direct fetch** (christmaslightemporium.com, christmasdesigners.com, christmaslightsupply.com, yardmastery.com and others). Prices come from search-engine snippets and review/cost articles, and each one is cited. Check them before you quote them publicly.
5. **Two Ad Library queries were blocked by the session's permission system:** a search for "christmas light installation Baltimore" and a lookup of Christmas Light Emporium's *inactive* (last-season) ads. Local Baltimore/Towson installer coverage is therefore thin. Run both searches yourself at facebook.com/ads/library.
6. **Keyword search in the Ad Library is fuzzy and full of spam** (novel apps, Marketplace-style listings). I dropped anything that wasn't a lighting advertiser.

## 2. Patterns across the winners

1. **"Never hang lights again" / "stop climbing ladders" is the category's default message.** It is used by installers and permanent-light companies, not DIY sellers. 1,173 active ads use the phrase, and variants include "Retire Your Holiday Ladder for Good", "Stop Climbing Ladders Forever" and "Install once. Celebrate forever." **The ladder is the enemy in almost every competing ad.**
2. **Dollar-off offers dominate lead-gen:** $100, $200, $500, $750 and $1,000 off, 20% off, and a free $200 wreath. DIY sellers use percentage discounts (Christmas Designers "20% Off Lighting Kits", CLC "15% Off Minleon").
3. **Local-name callouts plus scarcity:** "Utah County Homeowners…", "Reno Spots Filling Fast", "New Hanover County Spots Filling Fast". Headline-level geo-personalization is standard.
4. **Deadline urgency starts in August:** Christmas Light Emporium's "Production Capacity Fills By Mid-October" (running since Aug 18) and AE Lighting's "Last Call for Christmas 2026" (Sep 25). **Your Nov 15 deadline is late next to these.** Competitors are already pulling demand forward.
5. **Durability and quality contrast:** "One Of These Survives December" (relaunched), "Colors Never Fade, Lenses Never Crack", "Lights that brave any storm", "Lights built to last decades."
6. **Neighbor envy / curb appeal:** "Outshine the Neighbors", "Don't let your neighbors outshine you", "Your neighbors will envy you."
7. **"Pro-grade" framing on DIY products:** "Professional-Grade Holiday Lights – Shop Now!" (CLC, the longest-running ad I saw at ~58 days), "Pro-Grade Christmas Lights" (Christmas Designers), and the Pro Christmas Amazon copy.
8. **Mechanics:** many headlines contain "|" separators, i.e. **multiple headline variants (dynamic creative / Advantage+)**. Big spenders (Govee, JellyFish, Skyline, We Hang) launch 10–15 near-identical ads a day, which means constant creative testing.
9. **Formats:** not observable through this tool. Govee's blank-headline ads are probably video/Reels, and Christmas Designers is running at least one multi-product catalog ad.

## 3. White space Roofline Kit can own

1. **"Cut to your house, labelled by location": nobody is advertising it.** DIY sellers push "cut-to-length" wire (so the customer does the work), pre-sized stringers, or footage-tier bundles. The one true made-to-measure player (Christmas Light Supplies) is Canadian and runs on a scanned PDF form. **Labelled runs ("Run C: garage peak, left") is a visual, demonstrable difference nobody else shows.**
2. **"You own it, you don't rent it."** Installers bill every year ($442 average; "from $595"). Only permanent-light companies talk about one-time cost, and they sell at $22–$35/ft installed. **The one-time, sub-$500, classic-C9 look sits between those, and nobody claims it.**
3. **Classic C9 look vs. "pixel dots."** Govee and JellyFish sell RGB tracks; their ads are all "AI-powered", "16 million colors", "color zones." **Nobody is speaking to the traditional warm-white C9 buyer**, the person who wants the house to look like a 1990s Christmas card, not a stadium.
4. **Renters, HOAs, and people who don't want holes drilled or tracks left up all year.** Permanent-light ads never mention this, and it's a real objection to their product.
5. **"No cutting, no vampire plugs, no screwing in 300 bulbs."** DIY competitors sell exactly those chores as features ("cut to any length with included vampire plugs"). **Your whole value is removing them.** Say it out loud.
6. **Geo-local DIY.** Installers use local callouts; no DIY brand does. "Towson/Baltimore County homeowners" is a credible, cheap test.

**Offer tests to pair with these:** free photocell timer on orders over $X (installers give "free wreath"-style add-ons), or a small dollar-off ("$25 off before Oct 31") rather than a percentage. Dollar amounts dominate the winning installer headlines. Also add a guarantee: "every run fits or we re-cut it free." Nobody offers that, and it directly removes the "what if I measured wrong" objection.

## 6. Hard truths

1. **Govee sells a permanent, app-controlled 100 ft roofline system for about $200–300 at roughly the same per-foot price as you, and it never comes down.** Govee has 388 active ads and an affiliate advertorial network running "Tired of hanging lights every season?" A shopper comparing per-foot prices may see Roofline Kit as the *more expensive, more work* option. You have to win on the **classic C9 look**, **no permanent track on the house (renters/HOAs)**, and **"it fits your exact roofline"** (Govee can't be cut). If you can't make those points land in 3 seconds, Govee wins the click.
2. **Your exact model already exists** (Christmas Light Supplies, Canada: made-to-measure kits with clips, cords and timers, 7–14 days). It isn't advertising in the US that I could find, but "nobody has thought of this" isn't true. Your edge has to be the **ordering experience** (instant price, no PDF, no 3–5 day quote wait) and the **labelling**.
3. **Christmas Light Emporium is closest in spirit** (roofline bundles sized to your footage, "made to order", "production capacity fills by mid-October") and has run those ads for ~40 days. If their made-to-order line is roofline and not decorative displays, they are a direct, better-funded competitor. **Check their snapshots.**
4. **Your Nov 15 deadline is late.** Competitors started urgency messaging in August. Most customers who want Thanksgiving-weekend lights will have committed to an installer or a Govee kit by early November. Run a **"first-batch" deadline around Oct 31** and treat Nov 15 as last call.
5. **DIY C9 components are a commodity at $1.60–$2.40/ft.** If your ads don't make the cutting and labelling labor visible, you look like a 25–85% markup on Amazon parts.
6. **The whole category shouts "never hang lights again."** Roofline Kit still means a ladder once a year. Don't fight that message head-on. Use "measure from the ground in 10 minutes", "clip-up in an afternoon" and "no installer bill", and let the permanent-light and installer ads own "never."
7. **The measurement-error risk is your biggest conversion objection**, and no competitor ad addresses it for DIY. A "fits or we re-cut free" guarantee is probably worth more than any discount.
